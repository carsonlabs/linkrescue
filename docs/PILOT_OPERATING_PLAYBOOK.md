# LinkRescue Pilot Operating Playbook

This is a service-led pilot, not a self-serve SaaS launch. The goal is to earn the first recurring revenue through useful, personally scoped work while collecting evidence about what affiliate publishers will pay for.

## Daily routine: 30 minutes

1. Open `/dashboard/leads` and reply to every new lead within one business day.
2. Use **Draft personal reply** or **Copy reply**. Check the message, tailor one sentence to the site, and send it yourself.
3. Record the practical outcome directly in the lead inbox: `new`, `replied`, `qualified`, `scope sent`, `won`, or `not a fit`. Add a private next-action note.
4. Do not promise revenue recovery. LinkRescue reports technical evidence; traffic, merchant terms, and conversion are outside its control.

## Qualification checklist

Prioritize a prospect when all or most are true:

- The site has editorial or comparison content with outbound commercial links.
- The owner can identify the affiliate programmes or merchants they care about.
- The site is public and technically reachable.
- There are enough links or high-value pages to justify human review.
- The owner understands that a sprint is a fixed technical scope, not a revenue guarantee.

Politely decline or defer sites that are inaccessible, have no affiliate/commercial links, request guaranteed earnings, or expect work before agreeing on scope and price.

## First reply

Use the draft in the lead inbox and tailor it with one factual observation about the prospect's site. Ask for:

1. Important merchants or affiliate programmes.
2. Suspected problem pages or links.
3. Primary audience country or market.

The only purpose of the first reply is to establish fit. Do not attach an invoice, checkout link, contract, or promise of results.

## Follow-up after four business days

Send one brief, personal nudge only:

> Hello, just checking whether the LinkRescue review is still useful for your site. If you send the affiliate programmes and pages that matter most, I can tell you whether a fixed-scope review is worthwhile. No pressure either way.

Do not send a second automated sequence. Archive the prospect if they do not respond.

## Turning a qualified lead into the first sale

1. State the technical scope in writing: pages reviewed, link checks, redirect/tracking review, delivery format, and turnaround.
2. Quote the fixed Recovery Sprint price currently shown publicly: **$499**. Do not discount before learning why the scope does not fit.
3. Obtain written agreement on scope and price before accepting payment.
4. Deliver a concise repair list with evidence and implementation notes.
5. After delivery, offer the $149/month Monitoring Desk only when the site is a genuine ongoing fit.

## Weekly scorecard

Each Friday, count only real leads (exclude test addresses):

| Metric | Target for the first month |
| --- | ---: |
| New qualified enquiries | 5–10 |
| First replies within one business day | 100% |
| Scope conversations | 2–4 |
| Fixed-scope proposals | 1–2 |
| First paid Recovery Sprint | 1 |

If replies are low, improve the audience and the concrete site observation before changing prices. If prospects respond but do not buy, tighten the scope and clarify the delivery outcome.

## Guardrails

- Never add a visitor to marketing email without clear consent.
- Never send automatic sales emails from a lead form.
- Never make unsupported revenue or commission claims.
- Keep self-serve billing, automated scans, and recurring customer email disabled until the service workflow has been validated manually.
